How to Hire the Best Software Engineer in Bangladesh (2026)

By AJDM Jakiur Rahman • 12 August 2026 • 8 min read

Bangladesh has world-class engineering talent — but hiring the right person is still hard, because titles are inflated, portfolios are borrowed, and “full-stack” can mean very different things. This is the framework I’d use to hire a software engineer or agency here, written by someone who has been on both sides of that table.

First, define the problem — not the technology

The most expensive hiring mistakes start with “I need a Laravel developer” instead of “I need a subscription platform that bills customers automatically.” Lead with the outcome. A strong engineer will tell you when your assumed tech stack is wrong for the goal — a weak one just does what you asked and ships the wrong thing.

What actually separates a senior engineer

Years of experience matter less than what those years contained. Look for:

  • A documented promotion path. Someone who went from Developer to Senior to Team Lead was trusted with more responsibility over time — that is a real signal.
  • Production ownership. Can they deploy, monitor, and keep an app alive — not just write code that runs on their laptop? Code is half the job; DevOps is the other half.
  • Business reasoning. Do they choose architecture to solve your problem, or to show off a trendy framework?
  • Clear communication. Async-first, written updates, honest timelines. This matters more than raw skill on a remote engagement.

Full-spectrum delivery beats a pile of specialists

For most small and mid-sized projects, hiring five specialists — a backend dev, a DevOps person, a mobile dev, an automation engineer, a project manager — creates a coordination tax that eats your budget and your timeline. One engineer who covers the full spectrum removes that tax entirely.

That is the model behind Lunova Digital: Laravel and SaaS backends, WordPress plugins, Android apps, Python automation, and CRM/ERP systems delivered end-to-end, from database schema to production deployment, by the same engineer.

Red flags to walk away from

  • A portfolio with no technical detail. Screenshots prove nothing. Ask what problem was solved and how.
  • No mention of maintenance. Software is never “done.” If nobody talks about upkeep, you’ll be abandoned after launch.
  • Vague pricing. A professional gives you a defined scope and a fixed number, not “it depends” forever.
  • Overpromising timelines. An honest “this takes six weeks” beats a hopeful “two weeks” that slips to three months.

The five questions to ask any candidate

  1. Walk me through a project you shipped and kept alive in production. What broke, and how did you fix it?
  2. How do you deploy, and what happens when a deployment fails at 2am?
  3. How will you communicate progress, and how often?
  4. What happens after launch — how does maintenance and support work?
  5. Why is this the right architecture for my problem specifically?

The answers tell you more than any CV. You’re listening for ownership, honesty, and business thinking — not buzzwords.
